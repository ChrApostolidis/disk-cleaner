# Ballast logo brief

## Brief

- **Name:** Ballast. Ballast is the weight a ship carries low in its hull to stay steady, and the weight a balloon drops to rise. You can drop it when you need to rise and take it back on board later. The app does the same with disk space: it drops what can be rebuilt, and every cleanup can be put back.
- **What it does:** a Windows disk analyzer that recognizes dev projects and safely frees space that can be rebuilt (`node_modules`, `target`, ...), with a preview, the Recycle Bin and undo.
- **Feel:** calm and steady, sharp and precise.
- **Style:** no preference. The concepts show the range.
- **Palette (from the app, decided 2026-11-22):**
  - Ink: `#1c2027` light, `#e8eaee` dark.
  - Accent: `#2563eb` light, `#5b8def` dark.
  - Backgrounds: `#ffffff` light, `#15171c` dark.
  - The app's reclaim green (`#0f8a5f` / `#3ecf95`) stays a UI color.
- **Must work as:** the app and taskbar icon (Tauri icon set, down to 16 px), the README header, and the social preview (GitHub card and LinkedIn image).
- **Constraints:** the UI font is Segoe UI, which can't be used in a logo. The wordmark needs an OFL font. It replaces the default Tauri icon in `src-tauri/icons`.

## Territory

**Category clichés** (off-limits unless given a fresh form): brooms, trash cans and recycle bins, pie and donut charts, hard drives and disk platters, sparkles, vacuum cleaners, magnifying glasses, folder icons.

**Word map**

- *Ballast* → sandbag, ballast stone, keel, keel bulb, hull, waterline, hot-air balloon, counterweight, plumb bob.
- *Role* (knows what's safe to drop, lets you take it back) → release, rise, return, a tide that goes out and comes back.
- *Calm and steady* → horizon, level line, low center of gravity, symmetry.
- *Sharp and precise* → exact geometry, straight cuts, measured weight.
- **Where they meet:** the **waterline** (calm, nautical, level), the **keel bulb** (the literal ballast that keeps a boat upright), and a **bottom-heavy B** (the name itself, weighted low).

**Symbol approach:** Ballast is a real word with a clear image, so either draw that image in a fresh way or build the symbol from the letter B.

## Concepts

Longlist, one sentence each:

1. A circle cut by a level waterline, outlined above and solid below: the weight sits below the line.
2. A B built from two stacked blocks, the lower one wider: the name itself, weighted low.
3. A keel in profile: a flat-decked hull, a fin, and a bulb at the bottom, which is the actual ballast.
4. A balloon with one block dropping away below it.
5. Horizontal bars that get heavier toward the bottom.
6. A ballast stone with a slot cut through it as a handle.
7. A plumb bob hanging on a line.
8. A U-shaped hull holding one solid block at the bottom.
9. A square with one piece dropped just below the baseline.

Quick scoring: 4 reads as a person or exclamation icon, 5 as a hamburger menu, 6 as a kettlebell, and 7 is a construction cliché. 8 reads as a cup and 9 is too abstract to carry the name.

**Picked:**

| | Concept | Type | Signals | Rejects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Waterline**: a circle cut by a level line, outlined above and solid below | Abstract | Calm, balance, weight carried low and out of sight | Cleaner clichés, charts, anything busy |
| B | **Weighted B**: a B whose lower bowl is wider and heavier than the top | Letter-built | The name, solidity, stability | A blob that could belong to any tech product |
| C | **Keel**: a hull with a fin and a ballast bulb, seen in profile | Symbol | Steadiness, a real nautical story, engineering precision | Playful or cute |

## Round 2 (after feedback: A is the only good one, use the app's colors)

Longlist with fusions (two ideas in one shape):

1. **B with water in the lower counter**: the letter's lower bowl holds a water level in the accent. *(fusion: name + waterline)*
2. **Porthole with a rising bubble**: water sits low, one bubble rises above it. Both kinds of ballast at once: a ship's weight below, a balloon's lift above. *(fusion: ship + balloon)*
3. **Waterline B**: a B cut by a level waterline. The part below the line is the accent. *(fusion: name + waterline)*
4. **Ballast tank tile**: a rounded-square app tile with the water level inside. *(fusion: app icon + tank)*
5. **Settled weight**: a porthole ring with one accent weight resting low inside it.
6. **Balloon and released weight**: a circle lifting away from a small accent block.
7. Ballast stones stacked inside a ring.
8. A tilde wave inside a circle (water cliché).
9. A porthole whose waterline gap draws a B in negative space. *(fusion)*
10. A keel bulb alone, as a torpedo. Dropped: round 1 showed keel shapes read as tools.

Drafts 1-6 go to `drafts/` and the best three are picked by eye.
